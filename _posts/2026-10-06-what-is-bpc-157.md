BPC-157 is the most frequently cited compound in the research-peptide literature and the most frequently misdescribed. This note covers what it is and what has actually been studied, without the claims that usually accompany it.

## Structure and origin

BPC-157 is a synthetic peptide of fifteen amino acids with the sequence Gly-Glu-Pro-Pro-Pro-Gly-Lys-Pro-Ala-Asp-Asp-Ala-Gly-Leu-Val and a molecular weight of about 1,419 daltons. The "BPC" stands for Body Protection Compound, the name given by a group at the University of Zagreb to a protein they isolated from human gastric juice in the early 1990s. BPC-157 is a fragment of that protein, chosen because it was the shortest segment that retained activity in the group's assays.

The compound is made by solid-phase peptide synthesis rather than extracted from tissue. Two forms appear in the literature: the acetate salt and an arginate salt described as more stable in solution. Research suppliers typically list the acetate form.

## What the research has examined

The published work is dominated by one laboratory group and is almost entirely in cell culture and rodent models. Three lines of investigation recur.

**Tendon and muscle injury models.** Chang and colleagues (2011) studied the peptide in cultured tendon fibroblasts and reported effects on cell outgrowth, survival and migration, with changes in growth-hormone receptor expression. Rodent studies from the Zagreb group have used transected Achilles tendon, crushed quadriceps and similar injury models.

**Vascular signaling.** Several papers describe interaction with the nitric-oxide system and with VEGFR2 signaling in cell and animal models, which the authors connect to angiogenesis in injured tissue.

**Gastrointestinal models.** The earliest studies, consistent with the compound's origin, used rodent models of gastric and intestinal lesions. Later papers extended the same approach to a brain–gut axis framing.

What the literature does not contain is as important as what it does. There are no published controlled human trials of BPC-157. One Phase 1 study of an oral formulation is registered but unpublished. The compound is not approved by any regulator for any use, and the World Anti-Doping Agency lists it as prohibited in sport.

## How to evaluate a BPC-157 lot

Because the sequence is short and well characterized, identity by mass spectrometry is straightforward: the main peak should correspond to about 1,419 Da. Purity by HPLC above 98 percent is routine for a competent synthesis. The common failure is not purity but net content, where a vial labeled 10 mg contains materially less, so a certificate that reports fill weight is worth more than one that reports purity alone.
