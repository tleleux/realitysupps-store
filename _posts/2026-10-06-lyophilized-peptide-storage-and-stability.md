Almost every research peptide is supplied as a lyophilized powder: the compound is dissolved, frozen, and the water is removed under vacuum, leaving a dry cake in a sealed vial. The format exists for one reason. Peptides in solution degrade; peptides as dry powder, kept cold and dark, do not degrade quickly.

## What degrades a peptide

Four processes account for most loss of purity in storage.

- **Hydrolysis.** Water cleaves peptide bonds, particularly at aspartic acid residues. Lyophilization removes the water; moisture that re-enters a vial through a compromised seal restarts the process.
- **Oxidation.** Methionine, cysteine and tryptophan residues oxidize on contact with air. Vials are typically sealed under nitrogen or argon to limit this.
- **Deamidation.** Asparagine and glutamine residues convert to acidic forms over time. The rate rises with temperature and with pH in solution.
- **Aggregation.** Some sequences self-associate when concentrated or warmed, producing material that no longer elutes as a single peak.

Every one of these is slower at lower temperature, which is why the storage guidance for the sealed vial is simple.

## Storage of the sealed vial

| Condition | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Temperature | −20 °C for long-term storage. 2 to 8 °C is acceptable for short periods. |
| Light | Protect from light. Tryptophan- and tyrosine-containing sequences are light-sensitive. |
| Humidity | Keep the crimp seal intact. Do not open a cold vial until it has reached room temperature, or condensation will form on the cake. |
| Handling | Avoid repeated temperature cycling. Move a vial from freezer to bench once, not daily. |

Under these conditions most lyophilized peptides hold specification for twelve months or longer. The stability figure for a specific lot, where the laboratory has measured it, is listed on the lot's certificate of analysis.

## Shipping

Lyophilized powder tolerates the few days of ambient temperature a shipment takes. Cold-chain shipping is not required for the dry form and is usually marketing. What matters is that the vial arrives sealed, with the crimp intact and the cake dry and intact at the bottom of the vial.

## Signs a vial is out of specification

- The cake has collapsed into a glassy film or a wet smear.
- The cake has discolored (most peptides are white to off-white).
- The crimp or stopper is loose, or the vial was received warm after a long transit.

A vial showing any of these should not be used for research and can be returned under the sealed-vial policy on the [ordering page]({{ '/#ordering' | relative_url }}).
