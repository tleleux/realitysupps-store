Every product in this catalog carries the words "Research Use Only." The phrase has a specific meaning, and in the current enforcement climate it is worth understanding exactly what it does and does not do.

## The designation

Research use only (RUO) describes a product supplied for in-vitro laboratory research: work done in cell culture, in biochemical assays, or in analytical instruments. It is not a product category created by statute. It is a statement of intended use made by the supplier, and the supplier's conduct has to match it.

Under United States law a product becomes a drug when it is intended to diagnose, treat, mitigate or prevent disease, or to affect the structure or function of the body. Intent is judged from the circumstances surrounding distribution, including how a product is described, what is sold alongside it, and who it is sold to. The label is one piece of that evidence, not the whole of it.

## What follows from that

Because intent is read from the whole picture, a research supplier that takes the designation seriously behaves in recognizable ways:

- It describes compounds by their chemistry and by the models in which they have been studied, not by outcomes in people.
- It publishes no dosing, administration or protocol information, and does not sell the supplies used to administer compounds.
- It does not name approved drugs as equivalents.
- It verifies that purchasers are adults and qualified researchers, and refuses orders that indicate another intended use.
- It publishes independent analytical data for every lot, because the research use of a compound depends on knowing what it is.

The Food and Drug Administration's correspondence with research-peptide sellers since late 2024 has cited the absence of each of these practices as evidence of intended human use, regardless of what the label said. The designation protects a supplier that operates as a research supplier. It protects nothing else.

## Who may purchase

Reality Supps sells to qualified researchers and research organizations who are at least 21 years of age. Qualified means a person or institution engaged in laboratory research with the facilities to conduct it. At checkout each purchaser confirms this, and a first order requires a signed research-use agreement on file.

An order, a message or a social-media post indicating that a product is intended for a person or an animal is grounds for refusal and refund. This is not a formality. It is the mechanism by which the designation stays true.

## What the purchaser agrees to

The research-use agreement and the Terms of Sale cover five points:

1. Products are not for human or animal consumption, application, injection or any form of bodily administration.
2. Products are not foods, drugs, cosmetics, medical devices or dietary supplements.
3. The purchaser is a qualified researcher or is purchasing on behalf of a research organization.
4. Products will not be combined, mixed or otherwise prepared for administration to any human or animal.
5. The purchaser indemnifies the supplier against misuse.

## Why there is no dosing information here

Readers sometimes ask why a research supplier publishes compound chemistry and model summaries but nothing about amounts, frequency or route. The answer is the paragraph above on intent. Dosing information describes administration to a subject. A research supplier has no basis to provide it and every reason not to.
